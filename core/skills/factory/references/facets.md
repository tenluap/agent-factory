# FACETS checklist

Source: Iman Gadzhi's FACETS framework, from consulting.com's income calculator (`income-calculator.consulting.com/calculator/facets`, 2026-10-30) and its checklist card "The FACETS Checklist". The six questions below are verbatim. The guidance condenses each facet's section text and adapts it to projects as well as income methods.

"Six questions. Ask them every time."

## Checklist

| Facet | Question (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| F · Face | Can I run it without having to show my face if I wanted to? |
| A · Advantage | Can I start earning without spending years building my own audience? |
| C · Capital | Can I try it without risking much money upfront? |
| E · Expertise | Can I start it now, without having to learn any skills first? |
| T · Time | Can I succeed with it without having to spend several hours a day on it? |
| S · Scale | Can I increase my income without increasing the number of hours I work on it? |

"Anything less than a yes across the board, and you're looking at a method that might quietly take more from you than it gives back."

Yes = 1, no = 0. Total out of 6.

## Guidance

- **Face.** Yes when showing your face is optional and income does not depend on it. Content creation that needs you on camera is no. For a project: yes when it can earn without you as its public face or brand. `face_comfort` does not change the answer; it only changes how much a no matters in the summary.
- **Advantage.** The verbatim audience question is the primary test. A borrowed edge counts as evidence for yes: a specialised tool doing the heavy lifting, an existing asset, or distribution from `current_assets`. Competing on raw skill against better-equipped incumbents (day trading against banks and funds) is no, even when no audience is needed. Otherwise, when no audience is needed, the answer is yes: missing contacts, cold outreach and sales effort count under Expertise and Time, never here.
- **Capital.** Yes when you can start small and see quickly whether it works within `capital_at_risk`. Paying for inventory, product tests or ad spend before the first sale (dropshipping, Amazon FBA) is usually no.
- **Expertise.** Yes when a lack of expertise is not a dealbreaker on day one, judged against `skills`. Tools that do the expert part count. A skill that must be learned first (profitable ads, a new language) is no.
- **Time.** Yes when it fits `hours_per_week` and runs beside a job. Trading hours for money turns it into a second job: no. Months of work before the first result is no.
- **Scale.** Yes when both are true: it can grow into something very profitable, and it can eventually run without your daily input. Income tied to hours worked is no.

## Calibration

The source's worked examples. Use them to calibrate reasoning, not as answers: they predate the profile and some contradict the guidance.

| Method | F | A | C | E | T | S | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI dropshipping | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| AI trading bots | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| AI clipping | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 4 |
| AI voice agents | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

Dropshipping reasons from the source: customers never see your face (F1); specialised tools find winning products (A1); product tests and ads cost hundreds to thousands first (C0); profitable ads need expertise (E0); finding a winner takes months (T0); a winner sells to more people quickly (S1).

## Caveats

State the relevant ones in every result.

- The framework comes from a sales funnel for a paid programme, and its guidance favours "specialised AI" methods.
- The source key scores trading bots 5/6, yet its Advantage text says retail trading competes against banks with better tools. Judge from evidence, not from the key.
- The Advantage question asks about audience, while its section text is about borrowed advantage; see Guidance.
- Expertise and Time depend on the person, so a score is only valid for the profile it was judged against.
- A high score is a reason to look closer, not a forecast of income.
